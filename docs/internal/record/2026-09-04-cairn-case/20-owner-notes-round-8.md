# Owner notes for revision 8 (Geoff, 2026-09-04 and 2026-09-05)

1. Whole-system delivery is the regime the studies do not measure. Every published design keeps
   one human in the inner loop of one task and measures that loop's speed. The owner's regime
   runs implementers in parallel and overnight with gates instead of a reviewer in the loop and
   delivers whole systems in calendar days (the ASC layer; a Go server expected in four or five
   calendar days). Wall-clock there is bounded by machine and API. The absence in the literature
   is a lag, not a contradiction; the case says so and proves the regime by measured deliveries.
2. The measured build (record 16) is the first-party evidence: first pass 34 min 51 s, 198,039
   tokens, 113 tool calls, one commit, full showcase suite green; the review found it correct
   inside the dispatch's boundary and incomplete outside it (scaffold leak, workstation
   baselines, exemplar over doc, done-gate skipped, native validation dropped). The fix round to
   the published standard is being timed; its numbers complete the measurement as a two-pass
   figure. Both halves are stated with equal weight.
3. Individual experience is weak persuasion; the large-sample telemetry (record 17) and the
   documented practitioner and team records on cairn-shaped frameworks (record 19) carry the
   population claim: agents are the normal way this stack is now built, and the vendors' own
   data show they do not build it well without a gate.
4. The Cloudflare leg is one of cairn's strongest assets and is redrafted (record 18) under the
   heading the evidence holds; the lock-in counter is answered with the vendor's scale, the
   exits stated as facts, the setup carried by the scaffold, and ownership at every layer
   (repository, code, content, D1 export, R2 objects). The tie is the platform bindings layer;
   everything above it moves as-is.
5. Constraint Decay (record 19) is a greenfield study and cuts against the bare thesis; Leg 5's
   "no cited study measures greenfield construction" is revised, and the Lovable and Moltbook
