## 2. What the proposal does that none of them do

**Genuinely novel (no prior art found):**

- **Per-register cadence bands as a pass/fail gate.** The comma-hinged-pair share and the sub-eight-word share, banded per register, with a page outside the band failing. A hard search for prior art — academic, standards-body, and vendor — returned nothing. Style guides say "vary sentence length"; none defines a numeric band with a pass/fail threshold in a pipeline. Closest relative is GitLab's `ReadingLevel`, which is deliberately *not* a gate. This is novel, and its novelty is a cost, not a feature: nobody has debugged it for you.
- **"Author facts come from a brief," as a blocking finding.** No docs org in the sample has this rule. The nearest analogue is regulated-marketing [claims substantiation](https://www.commswith.ai/library/governance-approvals/claims-substantiation-checklist/) and governed [SME sign-off](https://zipboard.co/blog/techincal-writer/subject-matter-expert-sme-review-a-complete-guide/), where a named expert signs a locked version. This is the strongest part of the proposal and it is the part with the least ceremony attached. It is unusual in docs, not novel in general.

**Merely unusual, with prior art:**

- **Grading against named human specimens.** Standard practice in writing assessment as *anchor papers* alongside a rubric ([Marco Learning](https://marcolearning.com/writing-benchmarks-complete-guide/)); unusual in a docs pipeline.
- **Numeric sentence gates.** Red Hat (32 words + FK grade 9) and GitLab (25 words + FK grade 8) both already have them. The proposal's contribution is making them **block**, which is where both of those orgs deliberately stopped.
- **A fresh-context adversarial reviewer.** Anthropic's own [best-practices guide](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices) already prescribes this ("Add an adversarial review step"), and cairn already runs it.
- **Anti-AI-tell rules in agent instructions.** A whole genre now, e.g. [no_ai_slop_writing_rules](https://github.com/realrossmanngroup/no_ai_slop_writing_rules).

## 3. What well-run docs teams do that the proposal omits

1. **A published severity contract.** GitLab states which level fails CI, which shows in the diff, and which is editor-only, plus the policy that a new error-level rule requires clearing every existing violation first. The proposal introduces a blocking 40-word error and cadence-band failures without stating what happens to the pages that currently violate them, or how a legitimate exception is taken. cairn's register already has a "when a Vale finding is wrong" procedure; the new numeric rules get no equivalent.
2. **Measurement before gating.** GitLab's `ReadingLevel` uses `condition: "> 1"` specifically so every file yields a score for a **quarterly audit**. That is the mature move the proposal skips: it goes from zero cadence data to a blocking band in one step, on bands calibrated from a specimen set that does not yet exist.
3. **Testing the doc against a reader.** DigitalOcean's editor *executes the tutorial*. Kubernetes assigns a reviewer who is not the author. The [Center for Plain Language](https://centerforplainlanguage.org/most-readability-formulas-outdated/) — a plain-language advocacy organization — states that "reader-focused testing represents the gold standard," not formula scores. ISO 24495-1's test ("find, understand, use") is a **usability-testing** test; the proposal adopts the sentence and then holds it with sentence-length statistics instead of a reader.
4. **A reviewer who is not you.** Every org in the table has a second *person*: a technical writer, a paid editor, a PR wrangler. The proposal's "a person holds the reading" is the author reading their own draft plus agents. That is the same context that produced the front-door draft.
5. **Rules that teach.** Write the Docs notes linter adoption improves when the tool suggests rather than flags. The proposal gets this right (each rule names its plain-language rule) — worth keeping.
6. **Doing nothing structural.** Kubernetes ships large, well-regarded docs with no linter at all. The proposal never considers the option of fixing the process failure (a draft graded at the wrong path, no exemplar in the review dispatch) without adding a metric subsystem.

## 4. The three strongest arguments from the literature against readability-number gates

**(1) Optimizing the number does not move comprehension, and the best recent causal test found exactly zero effect.**
A four-arm RCT, n=2,235, rewrote health materials to SMOG-controlled grade 8, 10, 12, and 14 by varying **only sentence length and word complexity**. Mean knowledge scores: 9.0, 9.1, 8.9, 9.1 out of 14. "There were no significant between-group differences in mean knowledge scores... there is limited value in reducing grade reading level without attention to other health literacy principles." ([*JGIM* 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11606-024-09200-z), [PMC12119439](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12119439/)). Duffy & Kabance (1982, *J. Educ. Psych.* 74(5), 733–748) halved mean sentence length from 20 to 10 words across four experiments and obtained no comprehension effect. Klare's review of 36 formula-driven revision studies found about half succeeded, and those needed an average **6.5 grade-level** swing to move comprehension at all (Klare 1976, *J. Reading Behavior* 8, 129–152, via Redish). Eye-tracking evidence extends the failure to modern methods: traditional formulas, NLP readability models, commercial scorers, **and frontier LLMs asked to judge readability** are "all poor predictors of English reading ease in adults" ([Klein et al., arXiv:2502.11150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11150)).

**(2) Writing to the number can make prose worse, and the formulas' own authors say so.**
Klare's thermometer: writing to a readability formula "is like lighting a match under a thermometer to warm up a room... the room won't get any warmer," and "the temptation simply to substitute an easy word for a hard one, to cut sentences in half, or to eliminate prepositional phrases even when they are needed may produce exactly the opposite of the desired result" (Klare 1963, p. 25, quoted in [Redish 2000, *ACM JCD* 24(1), 132–137](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/344599.344637), [full text](https://redish.net/wp-content/uploads/Redish_on_Readability_Formulas.pdf)). Redish's paired example: "He is the defendant. He is fifteen years old..." scores better and reads worse than "The defendant is a fifteen-year old teenager who is accused of shoplifting." Charrow & Charrow (1979, *Columbia L. Rev.* 79(7), 1306–1374) improved juror comprehension by **adding** words that showed relationships between items, which made the readability scores worse. Davison & Kantor (1982, *Reading Research Quarterly* 17(2), 187–209) found professional adaptors' successful simplifications ran counter to formula recommendations. Bailin & Grafstein (2001, *Language & Communication* 21(3), 285–301, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0271-5309(01)00005-2)) make the theoretical case: length proxies ignore syntax beyond length, cohesion, and pragmatics. **This bites the proposal specifically**: shortening sentences to clear a 40-word ceiling deletes the connectives that make an explanation cohere, and the "comma-hinged pair" the proposal penalizes is often exactly the connective doing that work.

**(3) A measure that becomes a target stops measuring, and this one is trivially gameable — by the writer it is aimed at.**
Perelman's BABEL generator produced fluent nonsense that ETS's e-rater scored 5–6 out of 6 ([Perelman, eScholarship](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/263565cq)); the patched e-rater still rewards lexically complex nonsense. In the AI-text domain the same result is quantified: DIPPER paraphrasing drops DetectGPT accuracy **from 70.3% to 4.6% at a fixed 1% false-positive rate** ([Krishna et al., NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2303.13408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13408)), and detectors degrade against paraphrase in general with a theoretical bound on any detector's AUROC ([Sadasivan et al.](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2303.11156)). Prompt-only evasion — instructing a model to mix short and long sentences — is documented as sufficient ([arXiv:2305.10847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10847)). And the false-positive profile is ugly: perplexity-style signals misclassified **over half** of non-native-authored TOEFL essays as AI ([Liang et al., *Patterns* 2023](https://www.cell.com/patterns/fulltext/S2666-3899(23)00130-7)); OpenAI withdrew its own classifier at 26% true positive / 9% false positive. The proposal's cadence band is a burstiness statistic under another name, and the agent being gated is the one best equipped to hit the band without improving the prose.

**Add a fourth, cheap one:** nobody has shown a readability CI gate improved docs at a real org. A targeted search found only vendor content marketing; the one "28% task-completion improvement" claim is an explicitly hypothetical illustration on a docsie.io page, with no org, no data, no citation.

## 5. Concrete recommendations

**R1. Fix the citation before anything else. The Federal Plain Language Guidelines do not contain the numbers the proposal attributes to them.**
The primary source's "Write short sentences" section (III.b.1, p. 50 of the [March 2011 PDF](https://wid.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/FederalPLGuidelines.pdf)) reads in full: "Express only one idea in each sentence... Resist the temptation to put everything in one sentence; break up your idea into its parts and make each one the subject of its own sentence." **No 15–20 average. No 40-word cap.** Its sources are practitioner style guides (Charrow, Garner, Kimble, Redish, the SEC handbook), not word-count studies. The 15–20 figure is real but comes from a different publisher, [OPM](https://www.opm.gov/information-management/plain-language/) ("Sentence length should average 15-20 words"); the 40-word line traces to Cutts's *Oxford Guide to Plain English*, craft lore. Two further corrections: **plainlanguage.gov is archived and redirects to [digital.gov](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language)**, so "published, free, and stable since 2011" is wrong on the stability claim and the cited domain is dead; and ISO 24495-1:2023 is [deliberately non-numeric](https://www.iplfederation.org/iso-standard/) — the proposal invokes it for a "test" and then holds that test with numbers the standard declines to give.

**R2. Make every number a `suggestion` that reports, and gate nothing on it for one quarter.**
Copy GitLab's `ReadingLevel` design exactly: `level: suggestion`, `condition: "> 1"` so every page produces a score, feeding a periodic audit rather than a merge block ([gitlab_base](https://github.com/gitlabhq/gitlabhq/tree/master/doc/.vale/gitlab_base), [testing docs](https://docs.gitlab.com/development/documentation/testing/)). GitLab's own caveat is the right posture: "The score is not an exact science, but is meant to help indicate the general complexity level of the page." The proposal asserts bands ("19 to 44, best under 30") from a specimen set that does not exist yet; measure first, calibrate on real data, and only then argue about gating.

**R3. Drop the 40-word blocking error and the cadence-band failure. Keep them as reported measurements.**
Per §4 argument (2), a hard ceiling rewards deleting connectives — the failure mode Klare and Charrow both document, and the one ASD-STE100 itself warns against. Note also that the proposal's premise, "no gate we run has a rule about sentences," is **false for the editors track**: `.vale/styles/Microsoft/SentenceLength.yml` (`extends: occurrence`, `max: 30`, `level: suggestion`) is already vendored and active on `docs/editors/**` at `MinAlertLevel = suggestion`. That rule has been running and has not fixed anything, which is itself evidence about what a length rule buys.

**R4. Adopt GitLab's severity contract and migration rule verbatim.**
Publish, in `docs-register.md`, which level fails CI, which appears in review, and which is local-only — and GitLab's precondition that a new error-level rule requires clearing every existing violation first. Under that rule the 40-word error cannot ship, because the extend track is admittedly non-conforming. That is the rule working correctly.

**R5. Replace the "worst-band-first" rewrite ordering with a reader test.**
ISO 24495-1's own test is find/understand/use, and the [Center for Plain Language](https://centerforplainlanguage.org/most-readability-formulas-outdated/) states that reader-focused testing is the gold standard, not scores. Ordering a rewrite pass by distance-from-band is optimizing the proxy the literature says is invalid. Order by reader impact, and validate with one person who is not the author trying to do the thing the page describes — DigitalOcean's editor-runs-the-tutorial rule ([Write for DOnations](https://www.digitalocean.com/community/pages/write-for-digitalocean)).

**R6. Keep three items and drop the rest of the infrastructure list.**
Keep: (a) grading a draft at its destination path — a process bug fix with no metric attached; (b) the author-facts brief with a blocking finding, which is the one part with a real professional analogue in [claims substantiation](https://www.commswith.ai/library/governance-approvals/claims-substantiation-checklist/) and governed SME sign-off, and which addresses the actual defect Geoff named; (c) specimens named in the review dispatch, which is standard anchor-paper practice. Cut or defer the rest. Anthropic's own guidance is explicit that a bloated instruction file defeats itself — "If your CLAUDE.md is too long, Claude ignores half of it because important rules get lost in the noise" and "for each line, ask: would removing this cause Claude to make mistakes?" ([best practices](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)). The proposal adds rules to the global CLAUDE.md, the project CLAUDE.md, the output style, three register files, `tellgrader`, two hooks, `.vale.ini`, three agents, two skills, a workflow, and memory, for one failed page. The same guide also warns that a reviewer told to find gaps will find them; a cadence band is that failure mode encoded in CI.

**R7. Fix the arithmetic in "Decisions for you."** The list runs 1, 2, 3, 5, 4.

**One thing the proposal is right about and should say louder:** Google's Vale package has no sentence-level rule at all, which is why the front door passed. That is a real, verified gap ([errata-ai/Google](https://github.com/errata-ai/Google)). The fix Red Hat and GitLab both chose for it was an advisory measurement, not a gate.
