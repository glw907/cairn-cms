title: Starting a cold morning right

description: A note on why the first ten minutes at a frozen trailhead matter more than any mile after them.

The thermometer at the trailhead kiosk read eleven degrees when we pulled in, and the parking lot was empty except for one truck with its engine still ticking. Cold mornings like this reward a slow start. Boots go on inside the car with the heater running, because lacing frozen boots in the open air wastes ten minutes of feeling in your fingers you will want back later.

The first mile is the coldest part of the day, colder than it will be at the top, because your body hasn't caught up to the effort yet. Keep the pace easy and let the layers do their job instead of shedding them right away. Sweat now costs you warmth in an hour.

Frost had turned the boardwalk over the marsh section into a skating rink, and we walked the ice-free edge instead of fighting for traction down the middle. That edge line, a few inches of exposed wood, is there most cold mornings if you look for it.

By the second mile the sun had cleared the ridge and the frost was already dropping off the branches. We stopped there to peel a layer each, standing in a patch of light that felt twenty degrees warmer than the shade ten feet away. Small choices like where you stop to adjust make a bigger difference on a day like this than gear ever does.
