The gully on the north face has been freezing and thawing every day this week, the kind of cycle that loosens ice rather than locking it down. A frozen night sets a firm crust; by early afternoon that crust is dripping and the ice above it starts to let go in chunks the size of a dinner plate.

We stopped a hundred yards short of the gully and watched for ten minutes before deciding whether to continue. No cracking sounds, no fresh debris on the snow below, and we still turned back once the sun hit the upper rock band around eleven. The safe window on a day like this can close two or three hours earlier than the forecast implies, because the forecast tracks air temperature, not rock temperature in direct sun.

If the gully looks questionable, the traverse around its base adds forty minutes but keeps you out from under anything hanging above. We took that option and were glad to, since a chunk came down behind us not long after we cleared the runout.

Go early if you're set on the direct line, and build in the time to sit and watch before committing. An hour spent listening costs you nothing next to what a bad ten seconds under that gully could.
