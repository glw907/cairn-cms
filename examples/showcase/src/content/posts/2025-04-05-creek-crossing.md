Snowmelt off the upper basin has the main creek crossing running well above where it usually sits this time of year, fast enough that the standard rock-hop line is underwater. We watched two groups turn back at the ford rather than commit to it, which was the right call given the current.

A better crossing sits about a quarter mile upstream, where the creek splits around a gravel bar and neither channel comes up past the knee. It costs you the extra distance and a short bushwhack to rejoin the main trail, but it beats standing at the ford weighing a bad option against a worse one.

Cross in the morning if you can plan for it. Snowmelt runoff peaks in the afternoon once the sun has been on the upper basin for a few hours, so a crossing that's manageable at eight can be a different creek entirely by two.

Trekking poles helped more than usual here, both for balance on the gravel bar and for probing depth before committing a foot. The water was cold enough that a slip would matter more than the current itself. This should ease off within a few weeks as the upper snowpack works through its melt.
