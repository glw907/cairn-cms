Lupine and scarlet paintbrush have opened across the meadow loop about a week ahead of where they usually sit, likely a result of the mild winter and an early thaw at that elevation. The lower third of the meadow is thick with color right now; the upper section, still shaded by the tree line until midmorning, is a week or two behind and worth a second visit.

The trail through the meadow narrows to a single track in a few spots where past foot traffic has widened the edges into bare dirt. Staying on that single track, even where it means walking single file, keeps the damage from spreading into the bloom itself. A meadow like this recovers slowly from a summer of shortcuts.

Traffic on the loop has already picked up with the bloom, and the small pullout at the trailhead fills by mid-morning on clear days. Arriving before nine gets you both a parking spot and the meadow with fewer people crossing your photos.

The bloom typically holds for two to three weeks before the heat pushes it past peak, so this is close to the best window for anyone who has been waiting for the right week to make the trip.
