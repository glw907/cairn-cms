Sixteen hours of daylight this week opened up a loop we usually only manage as an overnight, the ridge traverse that connects the two trailheads on opposite sides of the drainage. Starting at five in the morning and finishing a little after eight in the evening left plenty of light to spare even with a long lunch stop at the saddle.

The ridge itself stays dry and rocky for most of its length, so the extra hours went toward moving slower through the sections worth lingering in rather than rushing the whole thing. We stopped for almost an hour at the overlook past the second summit, something you'd have to skip on a shorter-daylight version of the same route.

Water sources on the ridge are thin. We carried enough for the whole day rather than counting on the one spring near the halfway point, which turned out to be running but slow enough that filling a bottle there would have cost fifteen minutes we didn't need to spend.

A car shuttle between the two trailheads made the point-to-point possible; without a second vehicle, this becomes an out-and-back and loses a few of its best miles. Worth the coordination this time of year while the light allows it.
