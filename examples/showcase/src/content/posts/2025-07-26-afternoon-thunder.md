title: Reading the afternoon thunderheads
date: 2025-07-26
description: A predictable early-afternoon storm pattern this week means the ridge is only safe to be on before noon.
topics:

---
Every afternoon this week has followed the same pattern: clear skies until around eleven, cumulus building through midday, and a thunderhead over the ridge by two that's usually rumbling by three. It's predictable enough that the timing matters more than the forecast's percentage chance of storms, which has read the same thirty percent all week regardless of how close the storms actually came.

We started at six each morning and were off the exposed ridge section by noon, which kept us clear of the two afternoons that produced real lightning rather than just distant thunder. The ridge has almost no tree cover for the last mile below the summit, and that's the stretch to be finished with before the buildup gets serious.

Watching the cloud tops is a better signal than watching the clock. A cumulus tower that starts flattening out on top, the classic anvil shape, means it's already an active storm somewhere, even if it looks distant. That's the point to turn around regardless of how close you are to the top.

This pattern usually holds through most of the summer here. Plan the exposed sections for the morning and treat any afternoon start on this ridge as optional at best.
