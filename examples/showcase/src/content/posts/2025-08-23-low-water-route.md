title: Taking the low-water route across the flats

The creek that usually forces a detour over the footbridge has dropped low enough this week to cross directly, saving about a mile and a half of the loop. Water was barely above the ankle at the widest point, and the gravel bottom gave better footing than the footbridge's worn planks usually do anyway.

The flats themselves have dried out and turned dusty, kicking up enough grit on a windy day that gaiters are worth wearing for reasons that have nothing to do with mud this time. We noticed the dust settling into everything by the end, water bottles included.

Shade across the flats is limited to a handful of scattered junipers, so the direct crossing is worth doing earlier in the day before the open ground heats up. By early afternoon the reflected heat off the bare dirt made the last stretch before the tree line the least pleasant part of the whole loop.

This crossing will likely close again once fall rains bring the creek back up, so it's a narrow window. Worth taking if you're planning the loop in the next few weeks; the footbridge detour will be back as the default option soon enough.
