Rifle season opened on the ridge this week, and the usual loop crosses through an area that's open to hunting for the next several weeks. We wore blaze orange on top and on the pack, which isn't required for hikers here but is a cheap way to be seen at a distance in the trees.

Traffic on the trail changes during this stretch. We passed two hunting parties in trucks at the trailhead before six, both parked in spots we'd usually have to ourselves, and heard rifle shots from the ridge to the north through most of the morning, distant but clear enough to place roughly where they came from.

Staying on the marked trail matters more than usual right now, since it keeps you visible and predictable to anyone glassing the slopes above. The temptation to cut cross-country to a viewpoint is one to skip until the season closes.

None of this closes the trail, and most days it means little more than sharing the woods with people doing something different than we were. A little orange and a bit more attention to where the shots are coming from is enough to make the loop work through the next few weeks.
