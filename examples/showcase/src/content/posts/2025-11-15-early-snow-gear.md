The first real snow of the season came down heavier than forecast, six inches at the trailhead and closer to a foot by the saddle, and it sorted out fast which pieces of kit in my pack were actually doing something. Gaiters, which had ridden along unused since spring, were on within the first quarter mile and stayed on the whole day.

Trekking poles turned out to matter more than usual, mostly for probing what was under the snow on the steeper sections where the trail disappears into a uniform white slope. A rock or a root hidden under six inches of fresh snow is a different problem than the same rock in plain sight.

My gloves, a lighter pair meant for shoulder-season mornings, were not enough once the snow started sticking to them on the climb. A spare heavier pair rode in the pack for exactly this reason and made the second half of the day workable.

What didn't earn its spot: a rain shell that never came out, since the snow was dry enough to brush off rather than soak in. That's one item lighter for the next trip out, now that the first real snow has drawn the line between what gets used and what just rides along.
