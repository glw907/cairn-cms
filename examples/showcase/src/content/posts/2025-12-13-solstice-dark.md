Daylight has dropped under nine hours this close to the solstice, and the ridge loop that takes about six hours in summer leaves almost no room for a wrong turn or a slow stretch before dark catches up. We started in headlamps at seven and finished the last mile in them too, with barely six hours of real daylight in between.

The trail junction below the second summit is easy to miss even in good light, since the sign there faces the wrong direction for anyone coming from our side. In low winter light it's easier still to walk past, and we've done it before. This time we marked the spot on the way up so there'd be no guessing on the way down.

Cold slows everything down this time of year, from tying boot laces to eating lunch without stopping long enough to get chilled. Build in more time than the summer pace suggests, not less, even though the miles are the same.

A headlamp with a fresh battery in the pack, not just the one clipped to your head, is worth the extra few ounces on a day this short. Ours ran low on the last descent, and there was no daylight left to fall back on if it had failed outright.
