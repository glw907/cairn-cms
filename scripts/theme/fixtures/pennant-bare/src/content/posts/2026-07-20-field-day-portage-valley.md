We drove out on the Seward Highway to Portage Valley for July's field day. Twenty-two members came, six of them from the junior program.

Each pair carried a copy of the 1951 USGS Seward sheet at 1:250,000. At that scale, an inch on the map is about four miles on the ground, so the sheet shows the large shapes and leaves out the small ones.

Portage Glacier is next to Turnagain Arm, 50 miles southeast of downtown Anchorage. Hundreds of years ago it filled the whole valley, all 14 miles of it.

The exercise was the one we use on every field day. Each pair picked three labeled points on the sheet, took a bearing to each from the map, and checked it with a compass. The six junior members each held the compass for at least one bearing. The field-day lead's notes are on the lending shelf for anyone who wants to try the same exercise on another sheet.
